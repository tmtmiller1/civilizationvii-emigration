==== 4.0.0 | 2026-10-27 | src a2c61cc15b7c | gen 59ba377714f4 ====
[b]Version 4.0.0 · 27 October 2026[/b]
The Emigration screen is rebuilt around your settlements, displaced people come home on their own, wars treat refugees more sensibly, and the AI plays its side of the system. Your saves and settings carry over.

[b]Added[/b]
• The Settlements tab is the dashboard, and the screen opens on it. Each settlement gets a card with four panes (Arriving, Leaving, Arrived, Left) with the prosperity score between them, listing its terms.
• A departure forecast under the pressure gauge and in the city readout: “About 3 people are likely to leave in the next 4 turns”, or “Nobody is likely to leave soon”.
• “Dissuade newcomers” on each settlement's card, for 1 Influence a turn. People who would have moved there choose again: another of your settlements, another civilization's, or staying home, so you can lose them. People already on the road still arrive. AI civilizations dissuade newcomers in an overwhelmed settlement too, and pay the same upkeep.
• “All civilizations” shows each civilization's causes and a table of its settlements.
• People driven from one of your settlements into another can go home on their own once home is calm, and settle the plot their people left. Not all of them do: the longer they've been away, or the better off they are where they live now, the more of them stay. AI civilizations follow the same rule.
• Hasten and Call from abroad are buttons on the card, priced in Gold or Influence, with what the fee buys shown beside them.
• A small reminder with a homecoming quote replaces the call-home pop-up.
• A first-loss explainer for new players, off by default.
• The city readout says what would stop people leaving and what newcomers cost you.
• A notice when a civilization you've met opens or closes its borders.
• Spoil Guard hides civilizations you haven't met.
• A second AI city strategy, “hold your people”, and a price discount for the AI on harder difficulties (20% at Deity).

[b]Changed[/b]
• A settlement's Happiness counts by its own stage, the one on its banner, not against the average of every settlement in the game.
• Unhappy settlements lose people faster, the unhappier they are, as long as there's somewhere better to go. A Happy settlement's Happiness doesn't push anyone out.
• The AI's two city strategies are always on; the game-setup option for them is gone.
• A siege nobody fights stalls after three quiet turns and doesn't kill anyone until the next attack.
• People flee fighting once it has lasted two turns; a settlement being razed still empties at once.
• Refugees steer clear of a settlement that's under attack itself.
• Talent Attraction costs 2 Gold per settlement (3 in Modern) for its Science. Consular Protection gives +3 Influence a turn (+5 in Modern).
• A settlement's Character comes from its own people.
• Four tabs: Settlements, Network, Policies and Notifications. The Guide's answers moved to the Civilopedia.
• All twelve languages, the new screen and Civilopedia pages included.

[b]Fixed[/b]
• The AI's difficulty discount was stuck at half price from King up. It's 4% a step now.
• The “hold your people” strategy never switched on.
• Some screen text was invisible because the game's font doesn't draw italics.
• Calling people home kept offering people who had already come back.
• A civilization's pies on “All civilizations” left out moves between its own settlements.
• The settlement score counted hidden marker buildings, such as Cultural Diffusion's and Partisans', as buildings worth staying for.

Details in the [url=https://github.com/tmtmiller1/civilizationvii-emigration/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md]full changelog[/url].

==== 3.6.0 | 2026-10-17 | src 44a62e04aa28 | gen 394c552c3dd1 ====
[b]Version 3.6.0 · 17 October 2026[/b]
Ten policy cards instead of twenty-two, each one readable at a glance, and a stance that pays for its own slot.

[b]Changed[/b]
• The policy cards are down to ten: the Pro-Immigration and Anti-Immigration stances in every age, and Talent Attraction and Consular Protection from the Exploration Age. What the twelve other cards did now lives on the stances. A Pro-Immigration Stance protects the enclaves in your cities (paid in full even at war with their homeland) and draws war and disaster refugees to you first; an Anti-Immigration Stance watches them (enclaves keep half their yields, a quarter at war). The line about unrest comes off the cards; it belongs to the Partisans mod, which still reads the stance.
• The Civilopedia's Immigration Policies and Treatment of Communities pages describe the ten cards.
• With the Partisans mod running, the stance now sets how fast an enclave's unrest builds, as the treatment cards did.

[b]Fixed[/b]
• The immigration stances never worked. The game answers “slotted” for any policy card the current age's data doesn't hold, so in every age the other two ages' copies of each card read as slotted too: a Pro-Immigration Stance always came with a phantom Anti-Immigration Stance, the two cancelled, and migration ran as if no card were slotted (the Influence and Production still paid). Watched on 2026-10-17 in an Antiquity game, where the mod read “no stance” with an Anti-Immigration Stance in a slot. The read now skips the cards the age doesn't have. The same phantom read emptied the treatment and attraction cards of 3.5.0.

==== 3.5.0 | 2026-10-16 | src 6c0e56adace0 | gen 1ee84cede38a ====
[b]Version 3.5.0 · 16 October 2026[/b]
How a host treats the communities in its cities, as policy cards.

[b]Added[/b]
• Community treatment cards in every age, open to every civilization. The protective cards (Guest Right, Charter of Communities, Equal Protection) give +1, +2 or +3 Influence a turn, and an enclave you recognize while you're at war with its homeland is paid in full instead of half. The suspicion cards (Watched Quarters, Oaths of Loyalty, Registration of Residents) give +1, +2 or +3 Production in every city; in exchange, every enclave you recognize pays half and 25% fewer immigrants arrive. They unlock at Citizenship, Economics and Social Question, with the stance cards.
• Consular Protection, in the Exploration Age (Diplomatic Service) and the Modern Age (Political Theory): +1 Influence a turn for each recognized enclave of your people in another civilization's cities, up to +4. When a civilization hosting one of those enclaves slots a suspicion card, you hold grievances against it, once per host per age. The game records the host as the one who wronged you.
• When war first reaches an enclave, the Chronicle names the host's card: a suspicion card falls hardest on the families, and a protective one holds.
• With the Partisans mod running, people fleeing the fighting near an uprising are told as fleeing that uprising, and an enclave whose settlement burned in one gets its own Chronicle line. Without Partisans, the game plays as before.
• A Civilopedia page, Treatment of Communities, and the new cards in the list of every card by age. In all twelve languages.
• The City Details readout now names an enclave's stage first, Foothold, Established or Recognized, then says what the next step takes (“Becomes an enclave at 30% of Mutsu (now 22%)”, “Recognized in 5 turns if it holds”) and what's holding it back, in plain words. The enclave tooltip, the map marker, the Diversity table and the Advanced settings descriptions use the same stage names, and a new Civilopedia page, How an Enclave Grows, walks through the stages, how an enclave ends and the limits. In all twelve languages. How enclaves form hasn't changed.

[b]Fixed[/b]
• An enclave now goes with its settlement when the city is captured. Before, the old owner kept the enclave's yields and its wartime strain for a city it no longer held.

[b]Removed[/b]
• Fourteen Self-Test buttons that had stopped working. They ran engine probes for the old buildable enclave, which left the mod long ago, so pressing one showed a “running” line and then nothing. The Self-Test screen keeps its checks and the actions that work: a migration pass, the enclave and refugee pop-ups, the real dilemma, the test notices, the dashboard and the diagnostics snapshot.

==== 3.3.1 | 2026-10-07 | src e0c7748a4046 | gen 94c6110647a6 ====
[b]Version 3.3.1 · 7 October 2026[/b]
Settings that survive a restart: the mod now carries the Tower Settings Keeper.

[b]Fixed[/b]
• Settings kept after a restart. Civilization VII reads back only the first entry in its mod storage, whichever one a mod asks for, so options set in one session could come back as another mod's data or not at all, and a mod saving its options could copy that data under its own name. Emigration now carries the Tower Settings Keeper file, which keeps every mod's settings inside the one entry the game reads correctly and repairs a store another mod has already put out of order, without deleting anything. Emigration's options now keep their values from one launch to the next, and so do other mods' options that use the shared settings entry.
• The file runs before any other script and changes nothing else in the mod. If several mods carry it, or the standalone Tower Settings Keeper is installed too, one copy runs and the newest build wins. Nothing to set up: existing settings carry over. Tower Settings Keeper: [url=https://github.com/tmtmiller1/civilizationvii_tower-settings-keeper]GitHub[/url], [url=https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3815023570]Steam[/url].

[b]Changed[/b]
• English text: a wording pass over some option descriptions, guide notes and quarter messages. No change in play.

==== 3.2.0 | 2026-09-29 | src fb9f40c2537a | gen 80546b0af1c5 ====
[b]Version 3.2.0 · 29 September 2026[/b]

[b]Changed[/b]
• A disaster only displaces people where it actually did damage. Every settlement in a disaster's radius used to take the full distress spike, whether or not the event touched it. Now a settlement sends refugees only when the disaster newly pillaged tiles it owns, so a settlement that is shielded from the event (a Dam or a Levee against a flood, the Khmer Baray) or one the event spared sends nobody. The disaster is still reported where it struck; it just did no harm there. The gate is the disasterRequireDamage default in emigration-config.js rather than an Options-screen setting; off, every settlement in the radius takes the spike as before.
• A flood reaches every settlement along its river. A flood damages its river's floodplain, which can run several tiles from the epicenter the event names, so a settlement downstream that lost improvements is no longer counted as untouched. Other disasters still strike the epicenter and the ring of tiles around it.
• Flood-like events added by other mods are weighed as floods. An event class that carries FLOOD as a whole word (a mod that splits floods into a major and a thousand-year kind, for instance) now takes flood distress weight instead of the generic default for an unknown event.
• Reads the analytics policy for its own seat. Demographics 2.7.3 publishes each seat's effective analytics policy under a per-seat key, because the shared game configuration is one document in a networked game and the single key held whichever seat wrote it last. Emigration now reads its own seat's key first and falls back to the shared key, so a guest's choice can no longer change what the host's Emigration tabs show. No change in single-player.

==== 3.1.0 | 2026-09-18 | src df38d76d6fb0 | gen abeef5538492 ====
[b]Version 3.1.0 · 18 September 2026[/b]

[b]Changed[/b]
• Every decision pop-up shows its yields on its buttons. The refugee, newcomer, call-home and Cultural Enclave pop-ups all put each choice's gains and costs on the choice's own button, with the game's yield icons and in one shape (“Welcome them in: +1 population, −30 Gold, −10 Happiness”), so you no longer match a list in the text to the button below it.
• Refugee decision buttons show their costs. “Welcome them in”, “Settle the frontier” and “Turn them away” each carry the population, Gold, Happiness or Influence they change; the separate list of costs is gone from the pop-up's text.
• Newcomer and refugee placement buttons show what you gain. Every choice shows the population it adds, and “Let the city settle them” also shows the yields of the tile the city will take, for example “+1 population, +1 Food, +3 Production, +1 Happiness”. The city's own bonuses can add a little on top.
• Enclave stance buttons show what each stance pays and costs. For example “Learn their horsemanship: +375 Science, −1410 Gold”. A stance you cannot afford is grayed out and says “(not enough Gold)”, and the pop-up says the stance is paid once, when you choose.
• Enclave stances pay a real, one-time sum. Recognizing a Cultural Enclave pays once, when you choose: Culture toward your current civic, Science toward your current research, Influence, or Gold. Stances that do not pay Gold cost Gold; Gold stances are free. The sums are sized to your own economy: a stance pays three turns of your income of that yield and costs two turns of your Gold income, never less than a per-age minimum (60, 150 or 300 by age, scaled by game speed). An Exploration empire earning 221 Culture and 705 Gold a turn is offered, for example, +665 Culture for −1,410 Gold. Before, stances paid a small +2/−1 each turn, and most of those yields (Food, Production, Faith, Happiness, and every cost but Gold) never reached the game.
• Other civilizations take the first stance they can afford.
• Enclave tooltips and city readouts no longer list stance yields each turn. A stance is paid once, so the enclave's tooltip names the stance it took, and its tile's own yields are listed as before.
• Every civilization's stances were rewritten around what they now pay, in all 12 languages. The enclave's tile keeps its art and its own yield.
• Enclaves recognized before this version keep their old per-turn stance yields.

[b]Version 1.1.0 · 25 June 2026[/b]

[b]Fixed[/b]
• Critical mod-compatibility fix. Emigration could wipe other mods' settings out of the shared options store. Mods share one modSettings blob (one slice each); when the game's UI layer handed back a momentarily-empty or unreadable copy of it, Emigration wrote back only its own slice, and, worse, reset the whole blob to empty whenever it couldn't parse it, deleting every other mod's saved options. Emigration now re-reads on an empty result, refuses to write when the shared store can't be safely read, only ever touches its own slice, and never resets the blob.

[b]Added[/b]
• Brush & Blade civ/leader tuning pass. Extended the per-leader/civ variance table (ui/emigration-civ-tuning.js, Algorithm C) to cover the expansion's new civilizations and leaders, with abilities read from Contents_1.4.1/resources/DLC. All nudges are bounded and only applied to genuine migration [i]outliers[/i], the goal is to prevent snowballing, not flatten civ identity. 8 leaders: conquerors who profit from taking cities pay more gold to absorb the spoils (Alexander, Genghis Khan, Edward Teach assimilationEase 1.2–1.25); Bolívar instead [i]integrates[/i] conquests cheaply (0.85); Toyotomi takes double damage defending so his cities also shed population faster under siege (warRetention 0.85); Himiko is a happiness/celebration magnet (happinessPull 0.85); Napoleon's FOOD_BANE base persona gets a small growth cushion (sourceBias 0.5); Sayyida al-Hurra's naval-garrison cities resist depopulation (warRetention 1.2). 12 civilizations: conquest economies (Assyria, Bulgaria, Ottomans, Pirate Republic) pay more to absorb spoils, Pirate Republic's inland-unhappiness also makes it a net [i]source[/i] (sourceBias −0.5); tall/few-settlement shapes are shielded from the density penalty (Carthage, Nepal, Qajar); fortification-defensive civs retain population under siege (Dai Viet, Sengoku warRetention 1.4); happiness/celebration magnets are damped (Heian, Silla, Ottomans, Qajar); and high-growth Shawnee gets a cushion so per-capita dilution doesn't bleed pop (sourceBias 0.75). Iceland, Tonga, Great Britain, and four leaders (Ada Lovelace, Gilgamesh, Lakshmibai, Friedrich) were reviewed and left neutral, no migration-relevant outlier. The whole layer remains gated by civTuningEnabled.
• Civ-tuning strength knob (civTuningStrength, default 0.7). A single global “flatten between civilizations” control that compresses every per-leader/civ profile toward neutral: 1.0 = the full table as written, 0 = fully flat (equivalent to the table off). It interpolates each field toward its own neutral, so relative ordering is preserved (the most defensive civ stays the most defensive) while the absolute spread (the gap that feeds a snowball) shrinks uniformly across base and expansion entries. The default 0.7 keeps each civ's character but trims the divergence ~30% as an extra anti-snowball margin; exposed as a Scope tunable (0 / 0.4 / 0.7 / 1) for dialing.
